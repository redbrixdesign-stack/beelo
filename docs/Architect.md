# Beelo — Architect.md (v2)

**Status:** This supersedes the original Architect.md / Product Package v2. It is written against a working prototype (AdvisorOS) that has been self-built and field-tested solo for two months, and against real source documents from an active franchise home-visit sales advisor (appointment cards, quotes, delivery notes, commission statements). Where the original document guessed, this one states what was confirmed, corrected, or dropped, and why.

---

# 1. Why this exists

Before any of this was built, the working stack was free, familiar tools: WhatsApp for customer contact, Google Maps for routes, a mileage app, an expense app, a notebook, a shoebox for receipts. It didn't hold up, for two reasons that turned out to matter more than any single missing feature.

**Every one of those tools assumes two free hands and a chair.** They're built for someone sitting at a desk with a mouse, willing to configure settings, switch between apps, and type. The reality of the job is standing on a ladder with a drill in one hand and sample bags on the other shoulder — often literally one working hand, sometimes none. A stripped-down mobile version of a desktop tool is still a desktop tool. This isn't solved by "mobile-first" in the usual sense; it needs a genuinely different interaction model — voice-first, single control, nothing to configure — because there is no second hand free to tap through a menu with.

**Every one of those tools looks backward.** A WhatsApp thread, a mileage log, a shoebox of receipts, a note from yesterday — all excellent at recording what already happened. None of them look *forward*. None of them warn you, before you knock, that there's no parking on this street, or that the job you're walking into means carrying two sample bags up three flights in the same one-hour slot the company's own software also booked your next appointment. Every scrap of information needed to prevent that was already generated by the advisor at some point — on a call, on a previous visit — but no tool ever fed it back at the moment it would have mattered.

That is the actual product category: not admin software that records the past, but something that uses what the advisor already said to warn them about the next ten minutes. Beelo exists because nobody had built that for someone on a ladder with one hand free and thirty seconds before the next knock — not because field software doesn't exist, but because none of it was built for this posture, this moment, or this pace.

---

# 2. Product overview

Beelo is a personal, offline-capable, AI-assisted operational-memory layer for **commission-based home-visit sales advisors** — self-employed people who sell and fit inside someone else's system. The advisor doesn't own the customer relationship, the booking software, the pricing, or the commission engine; a franchisor or product company does (the validating case throughout this document is a Hillarys-style blinds advisor). What the advisor has none of is admin support, a personal record of their own earnings, or any tool that protects their schedule or their pay.

Beelo helps that advisor capture job context hands-free, resurface what matters at the moment it matters, draft communications for approval, track mileage and earnings, catch pay discrepancies the company's own statement doesn't explain, and avoid the schedule mistakes that lead to costly on-site errors.

Beelo is **not** a CRM, dispatch platform, accounting system, or product-specification repository, and it does not replace the company's own booking/order system — it reads what that system already produces and adds the layer of personal protection and memory the advisor doesn't otherwise have.

---

# 3. Target user

**Commission-based home-visit sales advisor**, sole trader or single-person operation, working under a franchisor/product company's own booking, quoting, and commission system (validated case: Hillarys blinds advisor).

- 15–17 appointments a week, mostly during the drive between them.
- No office, no dispatcher, no admin support — the company's software serves the company, not the advisor.
- Books via the company's own app; that app has no notes field, no personal memory, no schedule-risk warning.
- Carries sample bags, measuring stick, drill, blinds — frequently down to one working hand, sometimes none.
- Paid weekly commission, calculated by the company, deducted for defects the company decides on and rarely explains in advance.
- Also works survey and service-call appointments the company assigns without a sale attached, which affect commission rate but aren't commission-paying in themselves.
- Currently manages the gap with a notebook, phone notes typed while driving (dangerous), and a shoebox of paper receipts.

A meaningfully different secondary case (the `independent` employment model, kept in the data model but not the primary validated persona) is a fully self-employed advisor with no company behind them, who bears the full sale value of any mistake rather than a company-imposed flat penalty.

---

# 4. Core problem statement

Two problems compound each other:

1. **Every existing tool is built for the wrong posture and the wrong moment.** Configuration-heavy, two-handed, desk-oriented software doesn't fit someone capturing information hands-free between calls, on a ladder, or standing in a doorway.
2. **Every existing tool looks backward.** None of it warns the advisor before a mistake happens — no parking on this street, sample bags needed for a third-floor flat, a ten-blind job booked into a one-hour company slot right before the next appointment.

On top of that, the advisor has no independent way to verify what the company's weekly commission statement says was deducted and why, beyond trusting a flat penalty line with a one-word reason.

# 5. Product boundaries

## In scope
- Hands-free, offline-first capture (voice, photo, screenshot).
- Lead tracking from the moment an appointment lands, through immediate contact, to conversion.
- Downtime-aware batch review of anything captured but not yet matched or structured.
- Schedule-risk warning based on blind count and realistic per-blind time, checked against the company's own booked slot.
- OCR of the company's own documents: appointment cards, quotes/receipts, delivery notes, weekly commission statements.
- Automatic detection of defective-order (DOR) deductions from the commission statement, with commission-rate cross-checking.
- Mileage logging against current HMRC simplified rates.
- Basic blind-measurement checks (recess/exact, width/drop/diagonal, tolerance).
- Weekly earnings visibility against a target, with an earnings gap.
- Draft follow-ups and booking confirmations, always user-approved before sending.

## Out of scope
- Multi-user or team features.
- Full CRM, dispatch, or scheduling-engine functionality (the company already owns this).
- Accounting, VAT, payroll, or Making Tax Digital.
- Product catalogues or specification libraries.
- Customer portal or automatic message sending.
- Replacing the company's booking/order system.

## Hard constraints
- No mandatory cloud sync; core capture always works offline.
- No feature requiring two free hands to operate.
- No hidden AI actions — every draft, extraction, or warning is reviewable before it does anything.
- No claim of AI completeness while offline.

---

# 6. Main user outcomes

1. Nothing said on a call or noticed on a job is lost between that moment and the next chance to review it.
2. The advisor is warned about a schedule or access problem before it becomes an on-site mistake.
3. Every commission deduction is explained and traceable to a specific job, not just a line on a statement.
4. Weekly earnings, mileage, and expenses are visible without manual reconciliation.
5. Follow-ups go out faster because the draft already has the right context.
6. The advisor stays in control of every external action Beelo proposes.

---

# 7. MVP principles

- Voice-first and single-control, because two free hands can't be assumed.
- Forward-looking, not just record-keeping — the product's job is to warn, not just to log.
- Capture before organisation; matching and structuring happen later, ideally during natural downtime.
- AI as drafter, never as decider.
- The company's own documents are the primary data source — read them, don't replace them.
- Zero-configuration value from the first job.

---

# 8. Why Beelo is different

A market check found a mature, competitive field-service-management space (ServiceM8, Tradify, Checker, Powered Now, Commusoft, BigChange) and a separate commission-tracking space (QuotaPath, CommiTrack, Repfabric). Neither fits this user, and the honest reason isn't that the market is empty:

| What exists | What it assumes | Why it doesn't fit |
|---|---|---|
| FSM software | The tradesperson owns the customer relationship, dispatch, and invoicing | A franchised advisor owns none of that — the company does, through its own app |
| Commission-tracking software | B2B, insurance, or real-estate sales, entered at a desk | No concept of a physical measurement, an on-site defect, or hands-free capture mid-drive |
| Both | Two free hands, a chair, willingness to configure | The job is done on a ladder, often with one hand free |

**The real gap:** nothing protects an individually-commissioned advisor operating *inside* someone else's booking and commission system, using a hands-free, forward-looking capture model built for the actual physical conditions of the job. That is a narrower and more defensible claim than "nobody has built this," and it's provable — AdvisorOS has been running against real jobs for two months already.

# 9. Technical stack

**AdvisorOS is validated behaviour, not a codebase to extend.** It was built with basic HTML/no-code tooling to prove the workflows fast and cheap, with no requirement to hold up to real scalability, security, or multi-tenant data isolation — RLS, encrypted storage, proper auth, and an offline sync engine are not things that get retrofitted onto that. Everything below is a ground-up rebuild on this stack, informed by what AdvisorOS proved works and where it broke, not built on top of its code.

- **Frontend:** React + TypeScript + Vite, PWA with Workbox.
- **Offline storage:** Dexie + IndexedDB.
- **Backend:** Supabase (PostgreSQL, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions).
- **AI orchestration:** Claude API for OCR, transcription, drafting, and memory inference — server-side only.
- **Maps/route:** Lightweight map layer (e.g. MapLibre) for display; a real distance/routing engine (e.g. OSRM or a commercial directions API) is required for actual clustering/savings calculations — MapLibre alone only renders, it doesn't compute routes.
- **Hosting:** Netlify (frontend).

---

# 10. High-level system architecture

```text
User → PWA frontend → local offline DB → sync queue → Supabase
                                  ↓
                           AI reasoning layer (OCR, transcription, drafting)
                                  ↓
                 Voice notes / document extractions / schedule warnings
                                  ↓
                         User review and confirmation
```

Core capture (voice, photo, manual entry) always works offline. AI extraction is queued and honestly deferred when offline — never claimed as complete before it's actually run.

# 11. Core entities and data model

### Advisor
`id`, `business_name`, `employment_model` (`company_advisor` | `independent`), `base_location`, `working_preferences`, `commission_rate_percent`, `vat_adjustment_percent`, `tax_reserve_percent`, `install_only_minutes_per_blind` (reference benchmark, ~16 min — not used for scheduling), `full_job_minutes_per_blind` (used for real scheduling, ~33 min, ideally learned per-advisor over time), `weekly_earnings_target`, `consent_status`, `created_at`, `updated_at`

### Customer
`id`, `advisor_id`, `customer_number` (company-issued, durable across every appointment type for that person), `phone`, `postcode`, `address`, `display_name`, `contact_preferences`, `history`, `status`

### Lead
`id`, `advisor_id`, `name`, `phone`, `landed_at`, `status` (`new` | `call_attempted` | `connected` | `no_response` | `follow_up_due` | `converted_to_visit` | `lost`), `contact_attempts_count`, `source`

### CallAttempt
`id`, `lead_id`, `initiated_at`, `outcome` (`connected` | `no_answer` | `voicemail`), `voice_note_id`

### VoiceNote
`id`, `advisor_id`, `audio_path`, `recorded_at`, `duration_seconds`, `trigger_method` (`manual_button` | `siri_shortcut` | `assistant_action`), `status` (`recorded` → `transcribed` → `unmatched` → `matched` → `reviewed`), `transcript`, `extracted_blind_count`, `extracted_parking_notes`, `extracted_access_notes`, `extracted_name_spoken`, `linked_appointment_screenshot_document_id`, `matched_visit_id`, `matched_customer_id`, `match_method` (`screenshot_proximity` | `manual_review` | `name_hint`), `lead_id`

Captured anonymous by default — no real-time speaker identification is attempted; matching happens later, during batch review.

### Visit
`id`, `advisor_id`, `customer_id`, `customer_number`, `appointment_number`, `job_code` (primary cross-document match key, format `[A-Z]\d{3}`), `order_number` (secondary, long-form, only present at fit stage), `appointment_type` (`sales` | `survey` | `fit` | `service_call`), `job_source` (`self_sold` | `company_assigned` — `company_assigned` fit jobs pay commission at rate-minus-2%, since the advisor didn't make the original sale), `date_time`, `time_slot_start`, `time_slot_end`, `status` (raw company string, stored as-is, not force-mapped), `contacted_customer`, `blind_count`, `pre_visit_notes`, `company_scheduled_duration_minutes`, `estimated_duration_minutes`, `location`, `source_document_id`, `source_env`, `outcome`, `outcome_value`, `discount_percent`, `commission_amount`, `notes`

**Outcome taxonomy:** `Ordered`, `Quoted`, `Needs to Think`, `Talk to Partner`, `Comparing Quotes`, `Too Expensive`, `Spec Mismatch`, `Not What They Wanted`, `Not in Range`, `Windows Too High`, `Customer No Show`, `Advisor Could Not Attend`.

**Lead source:** `Facebook`, `Instagram`, `TikTok`, `Google`, `Website`, `Referral`, `Leaflet`, `Show Home`, `Event`, `Walk-in`, `Other`.

### Document
`id`, `advisor_id`, `type` (`appointment_card` | `quote_or_receipt` | `fit_completion_receipt` | `delivery_drop_note` | `commission_statement` | `expense_receipt` | `dor_receipt`), `subtype`, `image_path`, `parsed_json`, `status`, `match_status`, `source_env`, `additional_notes`, `model_version`, `prompt_version`, `confidence` (provenance fields added in Phase 3 when AI extraction begins)

`fit_completion_receipt` is a distinct document from `quote_or_receipt`, sharing the same room-by-room layout but generated on the day of fitting: "Order Number" rather than "Quote Number," a per-line **fitted/replacement status** rather than a price, a total of £0.00 (the sale was already paid at quote/order stage), and — critically — a re-fit date/time booked at the bottom for any replacement line.

### FitLineItem
Extracted from a `fit_completion_receipt`. Distinct from QuoteLineItem (which carries pricing from the sale) — this carries on-the-day fit outcome per blind, and is the earliest possible signal of an incident, available the moment the advisor captures this document rather than waiting for the weekly commission statement.
`id`, `document_id`, `job_code`, `line_number` (matches the numbered line on the receipt — links back to the corresponding QuoteLineItem for pricing/description), `room`, `position`, `fit_status` (`fitted` | `replacement`), `refit_date` (nullable, set only when `fit_status = replacement` — becomes a new Visit)

**Workflow addition — fit-completion capture:** Receipt photographed on the day of fitting → every line extracted → any `replacement` line auto-creates a **provisional** Incident (unconfirmed, `resolution_status: open`) immediately, rather than waiting up to a week for the commission statement → if a `refit_date` is present, a follow-up Visit is created/proposed for that date, not left as text in a document. When the commission statement arrives later in the week, its matching `CommissionLineItem` confirms the provisional Incident and fills in the financial fields (`penalty_amount`, `dor_rate_at_time_percent`) rather than creating a second, duplicate Incident — match on `job_code` + `line_number`/blind count.

### Incident — full model
The Incident entity has grown beyond the original mismeasure/DOR case as more real evidence came in. The single rule that governs the financial branch: **only advisor-caused faults open the advisor to a penalty. Everything else is Hillarys' responsibility, full stop** — confirmed directly, not assumed.

`type`: `mismeasurement` | `wrong_colour` | `wrong_product` | `installation_damage` | `window_breakage` | `logistics_damage` | `theft` | `warranty_malfunction` | `other`

`cause`: `fitter_error` | `customer_error` | `supplier_error` (production fault) | `logistics_error` (damaged in transit) | `theft` | `product_defect` (post-fit warranty failure) | `accidental` | `unknown`

`counts_toward_dor` (bool, `company_advisor` branch) — **true only when `cause = fitter_error`.** Every other cause — production fault, transit damage, theft, warranty failure, customer error — is Hillarys' responsibility and shouldn't touch the advisor's DOR% or cost them anything, by explicit confirmation. If a real-world case ever contradicts this (e.g. transit damage attributed to careless handling by the advisor), treat that as `cause: fitter_error`, not a new exception to this rule.

Shared fields: `id`, `advisor_id`, `visit_id` (where the incident was *discovered* — may not be the original fit visit, see below), `customer_id`, `type`, `cause`, `cause_detail` (including `copied_wrong_room_measurement`, a specific recurring pattern), `discovered_at`, `description`, `resolution_status`, `photos`, `notes`, `commission_line_item_id`

Type-specific fields:
- `logistics_damage`: `logistics_leg` (`hillarys_to_advisor` | `advisor_to_customer`) — which leg of transit the damage happened on.
- `theft`: no additional fields beyond the shared set; raising the incident is the entire resolution — it exists to trigger a replacement, not to assign blame.
- `warranty_malfunction`: a product fails **after** installation, within Hillarys' 2-year warranty, and is typically discovered on a `service_call` visit that may be months or years after the original fit — not the same visit as the original installation. Add `original_fit_visit_id` (FK to the original Visit, distinct from `visit_id` which is the service call where it was discovered) and `within_warranty_period` (bool). Add `service_call_outcome` (`repaired_on_site` | `dor_raised_unrepairable` | `escalated`) — on a service call, the advisor acts as Hillarys' on-site diagnostician: either the fault is fixed there, or the advisor is authorised to raise a DOR themselves if it's unrepairable. This is a materially different flow from the fit-stage DOR: the advisor initiates it directly, rather than it surfacing later on a commission statement.

`company_advisor` branch (only meaningful when `counts_toward_dor = true`): `dor_rate_at_time_percent`, `penalty_tier` (`standard` £20/blind | `elevated` £40/blind), `blinds_affected_count`, `penalty_amount` (flat rate × count, independent of blind value; no remake cost — the company remakes and refits at its own expense).

`independent` branch: `sale_value_lost` (ideally linked to a QuoteLineItem), `client_agreed_to_remake`, `remake_material_cost`, `remake_labour_absorbed`.

### job_code format — revised
Originally modeled as `[A-Z]\d{3}` (e.g. `H342`). One document showed `H301A` — a trailing letter suffix not previously observed. **Validation regex is `^[A-Z]\d{3}[A-Z]?$`** — the optional trailing letter is accepted but its meaning (revision marker, multi-order-per-visit indicator, etc.) remains unconfirmed. Matching logic must handle both `H342` and `H301A` as the same job code base.

### QuoteLineItem
`id`, `document_id`, `room`, `position`, `description`, `range`, `colour`, `width_mm`, `quantity`, `unit_price`, `line_total`

### CommissionLineItem
`id`, `commission_statement_document_id`, `line_date`, `invoice_number`, `job_code`, `customer_number`, `customer_name`, `line_type` (raw company label, stored as-is), `commission_rate_percent`, `order_value_inc_vat`, `order_value_exc_vat`, `amount_inc_vat`, `amount_exc_vat`

### Trip
`id`, `advisor_id`, `visit_id`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `distance_miles`, `path_points`, `status`

### Expense
`id`, `advisor_id`, `merchant`, `date`, `amount`, `vat_amount`, `category`, `photo_path`, `source_document_id`

### MessageDraft
`id`, `advisor_id`, `related_type`, `related_id`, `draft_text`, `status`

### ScheduleSuggestion
`id`, `advisor_id`, `date`, `suggestion_text`, `affected_visit_ids`, `estimated_saving_miles`, `estimated_saving_minutes`, `schedule_risk_flag`, `status`

### MeasurementCheck
`id`, `advisor_id`, `visit_id`, `window_id`, `blind_type`, `fit_method` (`recess` | `exact`), `width_top_cm`, `width_middle_cm`, `width_bottom_cm`, `working_width_cm`, `drop_left_cm`, `drop_middle_cm`, `drop_right_cm`, `working_drop_cm`, `diagonal_tl_br_cm`, `diagonal_tr_bl_cm`, `diagonal_diff_cm`, `tolerance_cm` (default 1cm — confirm at build time; earlier drafts used mm, the working prototype uses cm), `is_square`, `passes_tolerance`, `notes`, `photos`

# 12. Main user workflows

**Lead capture and immediate contact.** Appointment lands → tracked as a Lead → advisor calls immediately, ideally hands-free via a platform assistant shortcut → no answer flips the Lead to `follow_up_due` and Beelo nudges a retry, since re-contact odds drop fast after a miss.

**Hands-free voice capture.** Call connects → advisor records a free-form summary hands-free → saved locally immediately, transcribed once online → stays anonymous, no real-time matching attempted.

**Downtime batch review.** Beelo watches for a schedule gap plus a backlog of unmatched captures, and proactively prompts a review session during natural downtime, matched via screenshot proximity or manual pick.

**Schedule-risk check.** Once blind count is known, `estimated_duration_minutes = blind_count × full_job_minutes_per_blind` is checked against the actual gap to the next booked visit.

**Booking confirmation.** On visit creation, Beelo drafts a message confirming the time and proactively asking about clear windows and parking, for user approval before sending.

**On-site capture.** Visit opened → measurement check → photos → outcome selected from the validated taxonomy → follow-up drafted if needed.

**Quote/receipt capture.** Photographed → line items extracted (room, position, colour, width, quantity, price).

**Fit-completion capture.** Receipt photographed on the day of fitting → every line's fitted/replacement status extracted → any `replacement` line auto-creates a provisional Incident immediately, before the commission statement confirms it a week later → any re-fit date noted on the receipt becomes a proposed follow-up Visit, not text left buried in a document.

**Service-call diagnosis and warranty DOR.** Service call visit opened → advisor inspects the reported fault → outcome recorded as `repaired_on_site` or `dor_raised_unrepairable` → if unrepairable, the advisor raises the DOR themselves on the spot (a materially different flow from a fit-stage mismeasure, which only surfaces later via the commission statement) → linked back to the original fit visit, which may be months or years earlier, via `original_fit_visit_id` → since this is Hillarys' responsibility by product defect, it should never populate the advisor's `counts_toward_dor`.

**Delivery drop note capture.** Multi-customer, multi-line-item single document — fans out into several matched updates from one OCR pass, structurally different from every other document type.

**Weekly commission reconciliation.** Statement photographed weekly → every line extracted → defect-reason lines (Mismeasure, Wrong Colour, Wrong Order) auto-create/link an Incident against the matching job code, no manual entry required → commission rate cross-checked against expected rate for the job source.

**Earnings and tax view.** Weekly earnings vs. target, earnings gap, mileage claim (HMRC AMAP — confirm current rate as a config value, not hardcoded), expenses, and a clearly-labelled tax reserve estimate — a full tax-liability calculation (income tax, Class 4 NIC, payment deadlines) is a materially different and more regulated claim than "mileage visibility," and should be a deliberate decision, not a default.

**Offline resume.** App opens offline → local data visible → queued items shown → sync resumes later, always honestly labelled as pending.

# 13. MVP feature list, phased

This is a rebuild, not a migration — nothing here assumes the existing AdvisorOS code or data survives. Two concrete failure modes observed in AdvisorOS should be designed against from the start rather than patched later: unvalidated percentage/currency inputs (it accepted a >100% discount and silently produced a £0.00 "sale after discount" on a real £783 sale) and silent geocoding failure with no fallback (base address unresolved, no retry path surfaced clearly). Build input validation and graceful degradation into the foundation, not as an afterthought.

**Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–2).** Auth, advisor profile, RLS-isolated data model (section 11), offline-first storage and sync queue, core Visit/Customer CRUD with the validated outcome taxonomy. Confirm cm as the measurement unit and HMRC mileage rate as a config value, not a hardcoded constant, from day one. **No AI, no provenance fields.**

**Phase 2 — Close the capture loop (Weeks 3–4).** Lead + CallAttempt + VoiceNote + downtime batch review. The highest-leverage, most differentiated piece — replaces the "forgot by the next call" failure mode directly.

**Phase 3 — Protect the pay (Weeks 5–6).** QuoteLineItem + CommissionLineItem extraction, auto-Incident creation from commission-statement lines, commission-rate cross-checking for company-assigned jobs. **AI provenance fields (`model_version`, `prompt_version`, `confidence`) added to schema here.**

**Phase 4 — Protect the schedule (Week 7).** Blind-count-driven schedule-risk check, integrated into the booking flow, plus booking-confirmation drafting.

**Phase 5 — Round it out (Weeks 8–9).** Delivery-drop-note multi-fan-out OCR, expense capture, DOR rolling-rate prediction display, export/backup, settings (HMRC rate config), onboarding, pilot metrics instrumentation.

**Week 10+ — Pilot launch.** Recruit 5–10 advisors, distribute via PWA install, weekly check-ins.

# 19. Risks, assumptions, and open questions

**Risks:** OCR accuracy across company document layout changes; wake-word/hands-free trigger feasibility varies by platform (Siri Shortcuts / Google Assistant App Actions are the realistic path — a custom always-listening wake word faces real battery and OS-permission constraints); sync conflicts from offline edits; model/inference cost creep.

**Assumptions:** the advisor has a smartphone with camera, GPS, and platform-assistant support; the company's document formats (appointment card, quote/receipt, delivery note, commission statement) stay reasonably stable; commission and DOR mechanics are broadly consistent across similar franchise models, though rates and formulas will need per-company configuration.

**Resolved during spec review:** only `fitter_error` opens the advisor to a DOR penalty — production faults, transit damage (either leg), theft, and warranty malfunctions are Hillarys' responsibility regardless of who discovers or reports them. `counts_toward_dor` defaults to `false` for every cause except `fitter_error`.

**Open questions:** does a DOR ever appear only as a commission-statement line, with no fallback receipt, in all cases (confirmed for the validated case, worth checking for portability to other companies); does survey ever generate its own commission line under any company's model; what's the right default for `full_job_minutes_per_blind` before enough real Visit data exists to learn it per-advisor; how portable is the job-code/DOR/commission-statement model to franchises outside home-improvement (cleaning, gardening, other visit-based trades) versus how much of this is Hillarys-specific and needs generalising; **what does the trailing letter suffix on some job codes (e.g. `H301A`) mean** — a revision marker, a multi-order-per-visit indicator, or something else — regex `^[A-Z]\d{3}[A-Z]?$` is hardened; matching logic handles both forms; **does a warranty-malfunction DOR ever show up as a commission-statement line at all**, or is it purely operational (replacement issued, no pay impact beyond the flat service-call fee already covered) — affects whether `commission_line_item_id` should ever be expected to populate on that branch; **is theft ever attributed to the advisor** (e.g. an unlocked van) rather than treated as blameless by default, or is "raise the DOR, no fault assigned" the only real-world case worth designing for.

# 20. Short development brief

Build Beelo as a solo-user, offline-first, hands-free-first mobile PWA for a commission-based home-visit sales advisor working inside a company-owned booking and commission system. Primary capture is voice, recorded one-handed or hands-free, matched later during natural downtime rather than in real time. The app must OCR the company's own documents (appointment cards, quotes, delivery notes, weekly commission statements) to extract line-item pricing and to auto-detect defective-order deductions with their stated reason, cross-check commission rates against job source, warn the advisor before a schedule conflict turns into a rushed on-site mistake, support basic blind measurement, and show mileage using current HMRC rates. It must not require two free hands for any core action, must not silently claim AI output is complete while offline, and must not replace the company's own booking system — only read it.
